# IRDAI Compliance for Insurance Sales

*Knowledge area: IRDAI compliance for insurance sales. Audience: insurance sales professionals in India. Draft for coaching use; items marked [Verify] need SME confirmation.*

## 1. Who regulates what

The **Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI)** regulates insurers and intermediaries under the IRDA Act, 1999. The core law is the **Insurance Act, 1938**. The sections most relevant to selling are Section 39 (nomination), Section 41 (prohibition of rebates) and Section 45 (policy not to be called in question after a period). [Verify: current wording after later amendments.]

In 2024 IRDAI consolidated many older circulars into **master circulars**. The key one for sales is the **Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests, 5 September 2024**. [Verify: exact title, date and scope.] When someone quotes "an IRDAI rule", check it against the current master circular.

Coaching point: you do not need to memorise section numbers. You need to know the behaviours they protect: honest selling, suitable products, accurate disclosure and fair after-sale service.

## 2. Who can sell insurance

- **Individual agent:** licensed, tied to a limited number of insurers. [Verify: current tie-up limits.]
- **Corporate agent:** a company or bank selling for insurers (bancassurance). Bank RMs must be trained and certified as specified persons.
- **Insurance broker:** represents the customer and can offer many insurers.
- **POSP (Point of Sales Person):** simplified licence for standard, simple products, with shorter training. [Verify: permitted products and training hours.]
- **Insurance marketing firm** and **web aggregator:** other registered channels.

Licensing needs pre-licensing training and an examination, and periodic renewal. [Verify: hours, exam and validity by channel.]

**Disclose who you are.** State your name, insurer or firm, and licence number, and show proof when asked. Customers can verify a distributor's authorisation. [Verify: IRDAI's public lookup.]

## 3. Need-based selling and suitability

1. **Fact-find:** age, dependants, income, loans, existing cover, goals, health, risk appetite.
2. **Need analysis:** for example, income replacement for a family of four. Ten to fifteen times annual income is a rule of thumb for term cover, not a regulation.
3. **Suitability:** for savings and investment products, check horizon, liquidity and risk comfort, and record why the product fits.
4. **Benefit illustration:** use the insurer-generated illustration. Never hand-draw projections.
5. **Customer Information Sheet (CIS):** explains key features, charges and exclusions in simple language. Walk the customer through it. [Verify: current CIS format.]

Offer the proposal form and CIS in the customer's regional language on request. [Verify: exact obligation.] A customer who signs what they cannot read is a claim risk.

## 4. Honest disclosure

- **Never promise non-guaranteed returns.** Illustrated rates are examples, not forecasts. [Verify: current illustrative rates.]
- **Separate guaranteed from non-guaranteed** benefits in plain words.
- **Exclusions:** the life **suicide clause** typically limits claims if death by suicide occurs in the first year. [Verify: period and amount payable.]
- **Health waiting periods:** initial, specified illness and pre-existing disease. [Verify: current maximum PED waiting period.]
- **ULIPs:** explain charges (allocation, fund management, mortality, administration), the **five-year lock-in** and market risk. [Verify: lock-in and charge rules.]
- **Health sub-limits, co-pay and room-rent caps:** say them aloud, because they cause most claim disputes.

## 5. The free-look period

Life and health policyholders get a **30-day free-look period** from receipt of the policy document. They can return the policy and get a refund. [Verify: applicability by product and policy term.]

The refund is the premium paid, less **proportionate risk premium**, **medical examination costs** and **stamp duty**. For unit-linked plans, fund movement is also adjusted. [Verify: exact deductions.]

Mention free-look in the sale. It is a customer right. Never discourage its use.

## 6. Proposal form and material facts

Insurance runs on **utmost good faith**. The customer must disclose all material facts: health, smoking and alcohol, existing policies, income, occupation.

- The **customer** fills in and confirms the proposal. **Never fill in health or lifestyle answers for the customer**, and never advise leaving things out.
- Read answers back. If a customer says "no" to diabetes but mentions daily tablets, correct the form.
- **Section 45:** after a set period from issue or revival (three years under current law), an insurer generally cannot call the policy in question, and repudiation needs specific grounds such as fraud or proven material misstatement. [Verify: period, conditions and exceptions.] Never say "after three years nothing matters". Fraud remains a ground.
- **Consequence:** non-disclosure found at claim time can mean repudiation, so the family gets nothing.

## 7. Mis-selling and prohibited practices

- **Misrepresentation** of returns, features, insurer or regulation.
- **Selling insurance as an FD** or guaranteed investment.
- **Rebating (Section 41):** giving a part of your commission or any inducement as a discount. [Verify: penalties.]
- **Twisting and churning:** moving a customer out of a good policy mainly for commission. Replace only if it clearly benefits them, with surrender losses disclosed in writing.
- **Forced bundling:** presenting insurance as mandatory with a loan or account.
- **Forgery:** signing for the customer, misusing OTPs or documents.
- **Pressure tactics:** fake deadlines, emotional pressure.
- **Cold calling:** respect the **DND register** and TRAI rules. Assume calls are recorded. [Verify: current rules and calling hours.]
- **Pre-issuance verification calls:** never coach the customer on what to say. [Verify: where applicable.]

## 8. KYC, AML, nomination and MWPA

**KYC and AML:** insurers verify identity and address under PMLA and IRDAI's AML guidelines. Collect genuine documents and flag unusual patterns such as large cash premiums, unrelated third-party payers or sudden early-surrender requests. [Verify: document list, e-KYC and cash limits.]

**Nomination (Section 39):** the policyholder names who receives the proceeds. A missing or outdated nomination delays claims. Capture full names, relationships and shares, and update after marriage or a child's birth. [Verify: beneficiary versus collector nominee rules.]

**MWPA Section 6:** a policy on a husband's life, endorsed for his wife and/or children, creates a trust. Proceeds are generally protected from his creditors. This helps business owners and borrowers. [Verify: process and limits.] Do not oversell it as protection against everything.

## 9. After-sale service

- **Servicing and persistency:** remind customers of due dates, help with revival and address or nomination changes. Persistency (policies in force at the 13th, 25th and 61st month) shows customers bought what they needed.
- **Claims support:** help the family collect documents and track the claim. Never promise an outcome. IRDAI sets settlement timelines once documents are complete, with interest on delay. [Verify: days and interest rate for life and health.]
- **Grievances, in order:** (1) the insurer's **grievance officer**; (2) IRDAI's **Bima Bharosa** portal; (3) the **Insurance Ombudsman**. [Verify: timelines, portal and claim limit.]

Tell customers these routes exist. It builds trust.

## 10. Tax and GST

- **Section 80C (old regime):** life premiums deductible within the Rs 1.5 lakh limit. [Verify: premium-to-sum-assured conditions.]
- **Section 80D (old regime):** health premiums, with higher limits for senior citizens. [Verify: limits.]
- **Section 10(10D):** maturity and death proceeds are generally tax-exempt, subject to conditions and high-premium thresholds. [Verify: thresholds and ULIP treatment.]
- Under the **new regime** these deductions mostly do not apply. Ask which regime the customer is in, and never lead with tax.
- **GST:** individual life and health premiums were **exempted from 22 September 2025**, previously 18 per cent. Insurers lose **input tax credit**, so the premium drop may be **less than 18 per cent**. [Verify: scope, e.g. group policies.]

## 11. Worked scenarios

**A. "Will this give me 12 per cent returns?"** A 35-year-old in Pune is considering a Rs 1 lakh per year savings plan. The advisor says: "I cannot promise 12 per cent. Rs X is guaranteed, and the rest depends on bonuses that are not guaranteed. The illustration shows example rates, not forecasts. If you want growth, let me show you other options and their risks." The advisor then walks through the CIS and charges.

**B. "Just fill in the medical section."** A 45-year-old in Jaipur has had high blood pressure for years and wants "no" ticked to avoid a loading. The advisor says: "I cannot do that. If it is hidden and a claim comes, the insurer can reject a Rs 1 crore claim. Let us declare it and compare insurers. A small loading is better than a rejected claim." The customer fills in the form personally.

**C. "The bank says insurance is mandatory with my loan."** A bank RM has told a home-loan customer a Rs 3 lakh single-premium plan is compulsory. The compliant RM says: "It is optional, and your loan approval does not depend on it. If you want cover, I can explain the cost, options and free-look period." The RM records that the choice was voluntary.

## 12. Common mistakes and coaching points

1. Using "returns" language for protection or participating products.
2. Filling in or "tidying" proposal answers for the customer.
3. Leading with tax savings instead of need.
4. Skipping the CIS walkthrough when the customer is rushed.
5. Suggesting a switch without a written cost comparison.
6. Offering part of the commission to close.
7. Saying "premiums are 18 per cent cheaper after GST".
8. Ignoring nomination until claim time.
9. Calling DND numbers or using pressure tactics.
10. Hiding the free-look period.

Coach the habit behind them all: **explain it so the customer could repeat it to their family**.

## 13. Practice questions

1. A customer asks if your savings plan is "as safe as an FD at 8 per cent". What do you say? *(It is insurance, not an FD. Separate guaranteed from non-guaranteed benefits, show the illustration, promise no rate.)*
2. A customer's wife offers to fill in his health answers. What happens? *(The customer must give and confirm his own answers; explain that wrong disclosure can lead to repudiation.)*
3. You would earn more commission by replacing a customer's old policy. What is the compliant approach? *(Replace only if it clearly benefits the customer; disclose surrender charges and lost benefits in writing; let the customer decide.)*
