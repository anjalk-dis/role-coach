# Building and Coaching an Agency Team

*Knowledge area: Agency development. Audience: agency development managers who recruit, license, activate and coach life insurance agents. Draft for coaching use; items marked [Verify] need SME confirmation against current IRDAI regulations and insurer policies.*

## 1. The ADM's job

An agency development manager (ADM) builds a business through other people. Your output is not your own sales; it is the **number of active, compliant, persistent agents** and the **quality of premium** they bring. The core cycle is: **recruit, train and license, activate, coach, manage activity, protect persistency and compliance.**

A common trap is chasing headcount. A team of 40 agents where 6 sell is weaker than a team of 15 where 10 sell.

## 2. Recruitment profile and sources

Good agents tend to share these traits:

- Local social network and standing in a community
- Need for income, and willingness to work in the evenings and weekends
- Basic communication skills and honesty
- Minimum education as required by regulation (typically Class 10 or 12 depending on the location [Verify])
- Resilience to rejection

Reliable sources:

- **Referrals from existing agents** who know the candidate. These stay longest.
- **Homemakers and part-time earners** with strong local networks.
- **Teachers, coaches, small shop owners, retired people** and others with trust in their area.
- **Young professionals** who want a second income.
- **Other financial sellers**, such as mutual fund or loan agents, looking to add a protection line.

Weaker sources: mass walk-ins who were promised guaranteed income, and candidates recruited with unrealistic claims. Be honest in the interview: "In the first six months, income is small and irregular. Most agents who succeed treat this as a business, not a job."

## 3. Licensing and training

An agent needs an IRDAI licence through the insurer to sell. The usual path:

1. Interview and documentation: ID, address, education, photo, PAN, bank details.
2. **Pre-recruitment training** of around 25 hours for life insurance (IC-38 or the current equivalent) [Verify current hours and course name].
3. **Online IRDAI-linked examination** after training [Verify current exam body and process].
4. Licence issuance and activation on the insurer's system.
5. Product and process training: sales cycle, term and savings products, needs analysis, proposal forms, IRDAI conduct.

Pitfalls: recruits who lose interest during the 3 to 4 week gap between interview and licence. Keep contact, give small tasks, and hold the first weekly meeting before the licence arrives. Never allow unlicensed selling.

## 4. Activation in the first 90 days

Research and field experience both show that most agents who do not sell in the first 90 days will not sell at all. Use a clear plan.

- **Days 1 to 7:** Complete the starter kit and make a list of 100 contacts. Set up the agent's mobile app and digital tools.
- **Days 8 to 30:** Learn two products well (a term plan and one savings plan). Run 5 joint calls. Aim for a first case.
- **Days 31 to 60:** Set weekly targets, for example 15 conversations, 5 fact-finds and 2 proposals. Review weekly.
- **Days 61 to 90:** Build referrals from first customers and aim for 3 to 5 cases in the period.

Define **activation** precisely, for example "at least one issued policy within 90 days" [Verify insurer's definition], and track it by recruit batch.

## 5. Joint field work and coaching

A joint call is where an ADM teaches. Follow a structure:

- **Before:** Agree the objective. "Today you lead the fact-find. I will listen."
- **During:** Let the agent lead. Step in only if the customer is being misled or the agent is about to make a compliance mistake. Model a part of the cycle, such as the close, if asked.
- **After (within 24 hours):** Give three points: one strength, one fix, one action. Be specific. "You asked about loans and dependants well. You did not ask about existing cover. Next call, ask it first."

Tailor the style to the agent: a new recruit needs demonstration; an experienced agent needs a challenge. Coach on **cases**, not on speeches.

## 6. Activity management

Productivity follows activity. Use simple weekly numbers: **meetings, fact-finds, proposals, issuances, premium** and **active customers**.

- Hold a **weekly review** (30 minutes) with each agent or in a small group.
- Track a leading indicator, such as fact-finds, along with a lagging indicator, such as premium.
- If activity is high but conversion is low, coach the sales skills. If activity is low, coach the habit and the prospecting. If both are low, check motivation and fit.
- Act early. An agent with no meetings for two weeks needs a call, not a warning letter.

## 7. Persistency: 13th and 61st month

**Persistency** is the share of policies (by count or by premium) still in force at a point in time. The 13th month shows how many customers paid the first renewal. The 61st month shows long-term quality [Verify IRDAI disclosure definitions].

Warning signs: policies lapsing at the first renewal, customers saying "I did not know I had to pay every year", and early claims within the 2-year contestability window, which often indicate non-disclosure.

What drives persistency:

- Suitable sales: right product, right premium, right term
- Affordability checked at fact-find
- Clear explanation of premium payment term
- Servicing calls and reminders before each due date
- Auto-pay mandates

Review persistency **by agent**. An agent with high new business and poor 13th month persistency is not a star; he is a risk.

## 8. Worked example: a 90-day recruit

Suresh, an ADM in Nashik, recruits Pooja, 29, a primary school teacher who knows many parents. She joins on 1 June.

- **Weeks 1 to 4:** Training, exam and licence are completed by 28 June.
- **July:** Pooja lists 120 parent contacts. Suresh makes 5 joint calls. In the second, Pooja closes a Rs 75 lakh term plan for a parent, premium Rs 9,500 a year [Verify indicative premium]. This is her first case, and Suresh celebrates it at the weekly meeting.
- **August:** She runs 14 conversations a week, 5 fact-finds and 2 proposals a week. Three more cases are issued, with a total annualised premium of about Rs 48,000.
- **September:** Pooja asks every customer for two referrals and gets 6 names. By day 90 she has 4 issued policies and is active.
- Suresh then checks the 13th-month renewal on her cases in the following year, and calls the customers before the due date.

An unplanned agent who never gets her first joint call is typically gone by week eight.

## 9. Compliance supervision of agents

The ADM is the first line of defence against mis-selling.

- **Review proposals** for plausible income, correct nominee details and realistic affordability.
- **Spot red flags:** many policies sold to one family with the same bank account, very high early surrenders, customers saying they thought it was a fixed deposit, and complaints about "free gifts" or guaranteed returns.
- **Check the pitch** in joint calls: Is the product explained correctly? Is term offered where it is suitable? Are returns shown only from the approved illustration?
- **Never allow** rebating (giving back commission to the customer), signing forms for customers, using wrong income details, or forging documents [Verify specific IRDAI rules].
- Escalate serious issues to the compliance team. Do not hide an agent's problem to protect the numbers.
- Retrain, warn or terminate depending on the severity, as per the insurer's process.

## 10. Motivation, contests and meetings

Money and recognition both matter. Good practice:

- **Reward behaviour as well as results.** Include 13th month persistency, number of customers, term cases and zero complaints in contest criteria.
- **Use short cycles.** A weekly or monthly goal works better than a yearly one for new agents.
- **Recognise publicly** at the weekly meeting: first case, best referral, best service story.
- **Do not reward premium alone.** A pure premium contest pushes agents to sell high-premium savings plans to customers who cannot pay them.
- **Check contest conditions** against IRDAI and insurer rules on incentives [Verify].
- **Hold a positive weekly meeting:** 5 minutes on success stories, 10 minutes of training, 5 minutes on goals, then go out and work.

## 11. Common mistakes / coaching points

- Recruiting for numbers, then losing 70 per cent in the first year.
- Promising "Rs 50,000 a month income in three months".
- Letting an agent start before the licence is issued.
- Coaching in a classroom only and never in the field.
- Praising high premium without checking persistency and product fit.
- Ignoring weak agents until the quarter-end.
- Contests that pay only on premium, which encourage mis-selling.
- Not training agents in term selling, so they default to savings plans.

## 12. Practice questions

1. A new agent has been licensed for 60 days but has no policy issued. What do you do? *(Review the activity numbers, schedule a joint call within the week, and help the agent close a first case.)*
2. An agent has the highest sales in your team but 13th month persistency of 55 per cent. What does it suggest? *(Possible mis-selling, poor affordability checks or no servicing. Review his cases and joint-call him.)*
3. Your regional head wants a premium-only contest. How do you respond? *(Propose including persistency, term cases and complaint-free conduct, to avoid encouraging mis-selling.)*
