# Selling Insurance in a Bank Branch

*Knowledge area: Bancassurance selling. Audience: bancassurance relationship managers selling life insurance to a partner bank's customers. Draft for coaching use; items marked [Verify] need SME confirmation against current IRDAI, RBI and bank policies.*

## 1. How bancassurance works

In bancassurance, a bank acts as a corporate agent (or distributes under a similar arrangement) for one or more insurers [Verify current IRDAI rules on the number of insurers a bank may tie up with]. The bank has trust, footfall and customer data. The insurer provides products, underwriting and claims. The **bancassurance RM** sits between them: employed by the insurer, working inside the bank's branches, and accountable to both.

Two things make this setting different from field agency:

- **Customers trust the bank, and may assume the policy is a bank product.** That trust carries a duty not to mislead.
- **Customers come for banking, not insurance.** You must earn the right to start the conversation.

**Insurance is not a deposit and is not guaranteed by the bank.** Customers must understand this clearly [Verify exact disclosure wording required by IRDAI/RBI].

## 2. Using bank relationship data to spot needs

Customer data held by the bank can only be used as permitted by the bank's policy, customer consent and privacy law [Verify the Digital Personal Data Protection Act position and the bank's internal rules]. Within those limits, relationship signals suggest where a conversation may help:

| Signal | Possible need | Suggested angle |
|---|---|---|
| Regular salary credit, age 25 to 40 | Income protection | Term cover |
| New home loan sanctioned | Loan and family protection | Term cover linked to liability |
| Child account opened | Education and protection | Term plus a goal-based savings plan |
| FD maturing, large balance | Reinvestment, tax planning | Guaranteed income or savings plan, if suitable |
| Retirement approaching | Pension and annuity | Annuity or retirement plan |
| Business owner's current account | Key-person and family cover | Term cover |

Treat these as hypotheses. A signal never proves a need. Confirm with a question.

## 3. Conversation openers

The best openers are short, relevant and optional. Avoid "Would you like to buy insurance?"

- **Salary customer:** "I noticed you recently started your SIP and set up auto-pay for the loan. Have you ever checked that your family can keep the lifestyle and the EMI if your income stopped?"
- **New home loan:** "Congratulations on the home loan. Many customers like to know how the EMI would be managed for the family in an emergency. May I show you a simple option, which is completely optional?"
- **Child account:** "Setting up a savings account for your daughter shows you plan ahead. Would you like to see how to protect those plans?"
- **FD maturity:** "Your FD is maturing. While you decide where to reinvest, may I explain a couple of options? There is no obligation."

Ask permission, keep to two minutes, and offer a follow-up meeting if the customer is busy. Never approach a customer who is in the middle of an urgent banking task.

## 4. Working with branch staff and managers

Branch teams generate most leads. They respond to RMs who help them, not those who only ask for referrals.

- Hold a short **weekly huddle** with the branch manager to review the previous week's leads and the plan for the coming week.
- Give staff simple **trigger phrases** and a one-line referral process. Make it easy.
- Close the loop: tell the referrer what happened, within 48 hours.
- Recognise staff in the bank's approved way. Do **not** offer personal gifts or side payments, which can breach bank and insurer conduct rules.
- Do not pressure branch staff to push insurance on customers who have just refused. Branch managers have their own banking targets and customer complaints can hurt everyone.

## 5. Positioning term versus savings plans

Do not start with the product. Start with the goal.

- **Term plan** is the right answer when the customer's main need is to protect dependants against loss of income. It has no maturity benefit in the pure form. It is also usually the cheapest way to buy a large cover.
- **Savings or guaranteed-return plans** suit a customer who wants a disciplined long-term goal, such as a child's education in 15 years, and understands the lock-in, the charges and the returns.
- **ULIPs** link to market returns. Explain the risk and the five-year lock-in clearly [Verify].

A bank customer often thinks of insurance as an FD alternative. Correct this gently: "A traditional plan is a long-term commitment, not a fixed deposit. If you stop paying early, you may get far less than you paid."

## 6. Loan-linked protection

A home loan is the most common trigger, and also the biggest risk for pressure selling.

- **Cover must be voluntary.** The customer must not be told, or led to believe, that taking insurance is needed for loan approval, a lower rate or faster processing [Verify RBI and IRDAI position on tying and bundling].
- **Separate the processes.** Ideally, the insurance discussion happens after the loan sanction decision, or by a different person from the loan officer.
- **Offer choice.** Explain the options: the lender's group credit-life cover, or the customer's own term plan. An individual term plan stays with the customer even if the loan is repaid or transferred; a loan-linked cover typically reduces or ends with the loan.
- **Record consent.** The customer's choice and signature must be their own.

## 7. Conduct rules in a bank setting

- **Disclose clearly:** you are selling an insurer's product, not a bank deposit. The product's risks, charges and lock-in must be explained.
- **Suitability:** recommend based on the fact-find. Document why.
- **No misrepresentation:** do not say "guaranteed" unless the benefit is actually guaranteed, or "bank-backed".
- **No tying:** do not link insurance to the loan, the account, a locker, a credit card or any other banking service.
- **No rebating or inducements** outside what is permitted [Verify].
- **Handle complaints** through the insurer's and bank's grievance process, and escalate promptly.
- **Respect Do Not Disturb preferences** for calls and messages [Verify].

## 8. Worked example

Meera, 34, works in Bengaluru. She earns Rs 1.5 lakh a month, shown in the regular salary credit. She recently took a Rs 60 lakh home loan with an EMI of about Rs 52,000. She has a daughter aged 3.

The RM spots the signals, but starts with a question: "May I ask, how would the EMI and your daughter's expenses be managed if your income stopped?" Meera says her husband earns Rs 80,000 a month and that they have no personal life cover.

Fact-find summary:

- Loan outstanding: Rs 60 lakh
- Child's education goal: Rs 40 lakh
- Family income gap: about Rs 1 lakh a month, for 8 years = Rs 96 lakh
- Existing cover: none
- Cover needed: about Rs 1.9 crore. The RM recommends Rs 1.5 crore as a starting point within her budget, rising later.

A Rs 1.5 crore term plan to age 60 may cost about Rs 15,000 to Rs 22,000 a year [Verify indicative premium with a current quote]. Meera chooses to take the individual plan and not the lender's group cover. The RM also explains that the plan has no bearing on her loan. She signs the proposal, discloses a past thyroid condition, and takes the medical test. The RM follows up after policy issuance, the first renewal and her annual loan statement.

## 9. Pipeline and conversion

Treat the branch as a funnel: **leads, meetings, fact-finds, proposals, issuances, renewals.**

- Track conversion at each stage by branch and by referrer.
- A low lead-to-meeting rate suggests weak openers or poor lead quality.
- A low proposal-to-issuance rate suggests documentation, medical or affordability issues.
- Review **13th month persistency** by branch. A branch with high sales and poor persistency signals mis-selling.
- Plan visits: spend time on branches with high relationship potential and responsive managers.

## 10. Common mistakes / coaching points

- Implying that the policy is a bank product or that the bank guarantees returns.
- Linking insurance to a loan, an FD, or a locker, even by hint.
- Selling a savings plan to a customer who mainly needs term cover.
- Over-using bank data, or calling customers at inappropriate times.
- Ignoring branch staff until you need leads.
- Closing the sale without recording consent and disclosures.
- Chasing month-end targets with customers who cannot afford a long premium term.
