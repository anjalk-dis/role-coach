# Selling Insurance over Phone and Digital Journeys

*Knowledge area: Remote and digital insurance selling. Audience: tele-sales and digital insurance advisors selling term and health cover. Draft for coaching use; items marked [Verify] need SME confirmation against current IRDAI, TRAI and insurer requirements.*

## 1. What is different about remote selling

On a call you have no body language, no brochure on the table and no second chance at a first impression. The customer can hang up in a second, and they often did not expect your call. Remote selling is won on **clarity, permission and pace**: say who you are, why you are calling, ask short questions, explain in plain words and make the next step easy. Every digital step you guide must also be compliant and recorded.

## 2. Call opening and permission

The first fifteen seconds decide whether you get two minutes.

- State your **name, company and purpose**, then ask for permission: "Good morning Mr Sharma, this is Neha from XYZ Insurance. You had enquired about a term plan on our website. Is this a good time for two minutes?"
- For a warm lead, mention the enquiry. For a cold lead, be honest that it is a sales call. Never pretend to be a bank, government body or "survey".
- If the customer says "not now", offer a specific callback time, note it and honour it.
- Avoid scripts read in a monotone. Smile when you speak; the customer can hear it.

Coaching line: "Permission is not a formality. It is the first thing that separates a helpful advisor from a nuisance caller."

## 3. Building trust remotely

- Use the customer's name and their words. Repeat back what they said.
- Speak slower than feels natural, and pause after questions.
- Be transparent: "I will tell you what the plan covers and what it does not."
- Offer a way to verify you, such as the insurer's official app or website, and share your advisor or agent details as required. [Verify: exact identification to be shared.]
- Never rush or pressure with "last day" claims that are not true.

## 4. Need discovery on a call

Aim for five or six sequenced questions, not an interrogation.

1. Who depends on your income? (spouse, children, parents)
2. What are your fixed commitments? (home loan, education, EMIs)
3. What cover do you already have, personal and through your employer?
4. What is your approximate annual income and age?
5. Any health conditions, smoking or tobacco use? (needed for pricing and honest disclosure)
6. What matters most: low premium, high cover, or flexibility?

**Worked example.** Rohit, 32, Bengaluru, earns Rs 15 lakh a year, has a Rs 60 lakh home loan, a spouse and a one-year-old child. A rough human-life-value check: ten to fifteen times annual income gives Rs 1.5 to 2.25 crore. Add the Rs 60 lakh loan and subtract existing cover and savings. Suggest a Rs 1.5 crore term plan to age 60 and show an indicative premium. [Verify: premiums vary by insurer, age, smoking status and health.] Then check health cover separately.

## 5. Explaining term and health on a call without visuals

**Term in two minutes.** Use four beats:
1. "Term insurance pays your family a lump sum, say Rs 1.5 crore, if you are not around during the policy term."
2. "If you live through the term, there is normally no payout. You are paying for protection, not savings." (Mention return-of-premium variants only if relevant and with their cost trade-off.)
3. "The earlier you buy, the lower the premium, and it stays fixed for the term."
4. "Your family can use it to clear the home loan and meet daily expenses."

Then ask: "Does a figure like that make sense for your family?"

**Health in three minutes.** Cover: what is covered (hospital stays), the sum insured, **waiting periods** in plain words, room rent limit or co-pay if present, cashless network and the free-look period. Say what is not covered. Use one rupee example, such as a Rs 5 lakh surgery, to anchor it.

Keep sentences short. Check in every few sentences: "Is this clear so far?" Do not read a feature list.

## 6. Guiding the online application

Many sales are lost between "yes" and "issued". Stay with the customer through the journey, ideally on the call or video.

- **Proposal form:** confirm name, date of birth, income, occupation, nominee and address exactly as per documents. Errors here cause later rejection.
- **e-KYC:** typically via Aadhaar OTP, DigiLocker or CKYC, and PAN. [Verify: accepted methods under current KYC norms.] Warn that the OTP goes to the customer's phone, and that the advisor must never ask for or note the OTP.
- **Disclosure:** the customer must disclose health, habits and existing policies honestly. Do not fill answers for them.
- **Payment:** guide to UPI, net banking, card or e-mandate. Explain whether it is a one-time or recurring payment.
- **Consent screens:** make sure the customer actually reads and accepts the declarations.

## 7. Tele and video medicals

Medical evaluation depends on age, sum assured and health history. It may be a tele-underwriting call, a video medical or a home or centre test.

- Set expectations on the first call: "After you apply, there may be a short health check. It helps us issue the policy and avoids disputes later."
- Advise the customer on fasting, the time slot and documents. Confirm a convenient slot.
- Stress honest answers. Non-disclosure can lead to rejection of a claim later.
- If the underwriter loads the premium or asks for more information, explain why, calmly, and do not argue or promise approval.

## 8. Handling drop-offs

Drop-offs happen at the form, e-KYC, payment or medical stage. Diagnose before you chase.

- **Payment page:** possible reasons are a failed transaction, an unexpected premium, a limit on the card or a need to consult the spouse. Call within the hour: "I saw your application was almost complete. Was there a technical issue, or is there something I should clarify?"
- **e-KYC:** offer to walk through again or an alternative method.
- **Medical:** reschedule at the customer's convenience and reduce friction.
- **Price shock:** revisit need and options, such as term length, sum assured or payment mode, without cutting essentials.

## 9. Follow-up cadence

A sensible, respectful cadence matters more than persistence.

- Same day: call, then a short WhatsApp or SMS with a summary and the resume link. [Verify: transactional versus promotional message rules.]
- Day 2 to 3: one more call at a time the customer prefers.
- Day 7: a value message, for example "your quote is valid until..." only if true.
- After three or four unanswered attempts over a couple of weeks, stop. Respect "do not call".
- Log every attempt and outcome in the CRM.

## 10. Call compliance

Remote selling is heavily regulated. Treat these as non-negotiable and confirm details with your compliance team. [Verify all items against current IRDAI, TRAI and insurer policy.]

- **Call recording:** calls are normally recorded and the customer should be informed. Never switch off or pause the recording during the pitch.
- **DND and consent:** check the National Customer Preference Register status before calling. Call only within permitted hours and with a valid basis, such as an enquiry or consent. Honour opt-outs immediately.
- **Disclosure:** state who you are, the insurer, that this is a sales call, and the product's key features, exclusions, premium and term. Say whether returns are guaranteed or not.
- **No misleading statements:** do not promise guaranteed claim settlement, claim ratios as a guarantee, unrealistic returns, or call a policy a "fixed deposit" or "investment" when it is protection. Do not compare competitors unfairly. Do not call a policy "government-approved" or "mandatory".
- **Free-look period:** tell the customer they can review the policy and cancel within the free-look period. [Verify: duration, commonly 15 or 30 days.]
- **Pre-issuance verification call (PIVC):** many insurers call the customer after the application to confirm key details, premium and understanding. Prepare the customer: "You will get a verification call. Please confirm the details honestly."
- **Data protection:** share personal data only through approved channels. Never take OTPs, passwords or card CVV.

## 11. Metrics that matter

- **Contact rate:** connected calls divided by dialled calls.
- **Conversion:** applications divided by qualified conversations.
- **Issuance rate:** policies issued divided by applications. This reveals drop-offs and underwriting issues.
- **Persistency:** share of policies still in force at 13 months, 25 months and so on. This reveals mis-selling. [Verify: insurer definitions.]
- **Complaint and cancellation rates:** free-look cancellations are a warning sign.

**Worked example.** An advisor dials 200 leads a day and connects with 60 (contact rate 30%). Twelve become applications (20% of connected calls) and seven are issued (58% issuance). The weakest stage is issuance. Lifting it to 75% through better drop-off recovery gives nine policies a day without any extra dialling. If the average annual premium is Rs 20,000, that is an extra Rs 40,000 of annual premium daily.

## 12. Common mistakes and coaching points

- Starting the pitch before asking permission.
- Reading a feature list instead of connecting to the customer's family and loans.
- Using jargon like "sum assured", "rider" and "moratorium" without explaining.
- Filling the proposal form on the customer's behalf or hiding exclusions.
- Chasing a drop-off with a pressure call instead of finding the cause.
- Promising the claim will be paid, or quoting returns on a protection plan.
- Ignoring DND and callback promises.

## 13. Practice questions

1. A customer says "I am busy, call later". What do you do? *(Thank them, offer a specific time, record it and call back exactly then.)*
2. A customer asks "Will the claim definitely be paid?" *(No guarantees. Explain the claim process, the importance of honest disclosure and the insurer's published settlement information.)*
3. A customer completes the application but never replies to the PIVC call. What is the risk and your move? *(The policy may not be issued. Reach out within the hour to explain the call's purpose and help them complete it.)*
