# Healthy Work Habits

*Knowledge area: Healthy work habits. Audience: employees at any level who want to manage stress and build healthier work habits. Draft for coaching use; items marked [Verify] need SME confirmation.*

## 1. Habits, not heroics

Healthy work habits are small, repeatable routines that protect sleep, movement, energy and focus. The aim is not a perfect routine. It is a routine that survives a busy week. A good coaching approach is to pick **one** small change, try it for two weeks, and then add another. This is general wellness guidance and not medical advice. Anyone with a health condition or persistent symptoms should speak to a doctor.

## 2. Sleep basics

Most adults do best with roughly seven to nine hours of sleep. [Verify: commonly cited adult range.] Sleep is when the body and mind recover, and poor sleep makes stress feel bigger, concentration worse and mood lower. Practical basics:

- **Consistent timing:** go to bed and wake up at roughly the same time, even on weekends, within about an hour. This matters more than any single trick.
- **Wind-down routine:** a 30-minute buffer before bed with dim lights, a warm shower, light stretching, a few pages of a book or writing tomorrow's to-do list so it is out of your head.
- **Screens:** try to put the phone away, or at least out of reach, 30 to 60 minutes before sleep. Work email and social media keep the mind alert. Night mode helps a little but does not fix late scrolling.
- **Caffeine timing:** many people find it helps to stop tea, coffee and energy drinks about six to eight hours before bedtime. [Verify: commonly cited guideline.] The chai at 6 pm may be the reason sleep is light at midnight.
- **If you cannot sleep:** get up after a while, do something calm in low light, and return when sleepy. Lying there watching the clock tends to make it worse.

Persistent sleep trouble for more than a few weeks is a reason to see a doctor. Do not suggest sleep aids or supplements.

## 3. Movement and desk ergonomics

Sitting for long stretches stiffens the body and drains energy. Small, frequent movement helps more than one long gym session squeezed into a hectic week.

**Quick workstation check, spoken step by step:**

1. Sit back in the chair with your feet flat on the floor or a footrest.
2. Keep your knees roughly at hip level, with your lower back supported.
3. Place the top of the screen at about eye level, an arm's length away. On a laptop, a stand and a separate keyboard make this easier.
4. Keep elbows close to your body, bent about ninety degrees, with wrists straight.
5. Keep the phone away from your shoulder. Use earphones for long calls.

**Breaks:** stand up, stretch or walk every 30 to 60 minutes, even for a minute or two. A timer or a calendar nudge helps. Standing for a call, or walking while on a phone-only call, is an easy win.

**Simple desk stretches (about a minute):**

1. Roll your shoulders slowly backwards five times.
2. Gently tilt your head to one side, then the other, holding for a few breaths each.
3. Stretch your arms overhead and lean gently to each side.

Everything should feel like a gentle stretch, never pain. If someone has ongoing pain, tingling or numbness, they should see a doctor or physiotherapist.

**The 20-20-20 habit for eyes:** every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet (six metres) away for 20 seconds. [Verify: commonly cited guideline.]

## 4. Energy and focus

Energy is not constant. Most people have a natural peak and a dip during the day, often a morning peak and a post-lunch dip.

- **Know your peak:** use it for your most demanding work, such as writing, analysis or design. Keep meetings, emails and admin for lower-energy hours where you can.
- **Time blocking:** reserve calendar blocks for focused work and treat them like meetings. Block "no meeting" time where the team culture allows.
- **Deep work:** work on one task for 45 to 90 minutes with email and chat closed, then take a short break. Switching between tasks repeatedly costs mental energy.
- **Notifications:** turn off non-essential alerts. Check email and chat at set times, for example at the top of each hour, instead of reacting to every ping.
- **Break well:** a real break means leaving the screen. Scrolling social media is not a rest for the brain.

## 5. Hydration and meals, in general terms

This section is general wellbeing guidance, not dietary advice.

- Keep a water bottle at the desk and sip through the day.
- Try not to skip meals. Regular meals with some protein, vegetables and whole grains tend to keep energy steadier than long gaps followed by heavy meals.
- Notice reliance on lots of tea, coffee or sugary snacks to push through the day. It can be a signal to look at sleep and workload.
- For specific diets, conditions or concerns, a doctor or registered dietitian is the right person.

## 6. Digital wellbeing and switching off

Many people are "always on" because phones carry work. Switching off is a skill.

- **Create a shutdown ritual:** at the end of the day, write tomorrow's top three tasks, close the laptop and say a phrase such as "done for today".
- **Separate the spaces:** log out of work email on the personal phone if possible, or mute work chat after hours.
- **Set response expectations:** agree with your manager what counts as urgent and how to reach you if it is.
- **Fill the gap:** a walk, cooking, music, family time or a hobby marks the shift from work to personal time.

## 7. Hybrid and remote routines

Working from home blurs boundaries. Help people build structure.

- Keep a consistent start and end time, and dress for work if it helps your mindset.
- Use a dedicated corner for work if possible, and leave it at the end of the day.
- Take real lunch breaks away from the desk, and step outside for light and fresh air.
- Schedule a few real conversations with colleagues, as isolation can creep in quietly.
- On office days, use the commute for something that helps you decompress, such as music or a podcast, rather than checking email.

## 8. Worked example: the sales manager with travel

Rohit is a 36-year-old regional sales manager in Pune who travels three days a week to meet distributors. He eats at irregular hours, drinks six cups of chai, sleeps at different times in hotels, replies to WhatsApp messages until midnight, and has not exercised in months. He feels tired by Thursday and is short with his team.

How a coach might help, picking small steps:

1. **Choose one anchor habit.** For Rohit, a consistent wake-up time, even on travel days, within an hour of his usual.
2. **Cut late caffeine.** Move the last chai to before 2 pm, and switch to water or buttermilk after that. [Verify: guideline is general; tolerance varies.]
3. **Movement on the road.** A 10-minute walk after landing or before the first meeting, and the stairs instead of the lift. A few desk stretches in the car or hotel room.
4. **Meals.** Carry a simple snack such as nuts or fruit to avoid skipping meals between visits.
5. **Switch off.** Set a message cut-off at 9:30 pm for non-urgent chats and tell his team the rule.
6. **Review in two weeks.** If he is still very tired, struggling to sleep or feeling low, suggest speaking to a doctor or the EAP.

None of this needs a gym membership. The goal is steadier energy.

## 9. Common mistakes and coaching points

- Trying to change everything at once. Pick one habit and build on it.
- Relying on willpower instead of setting up the environment, such as a phone charging outside the bedroom.
- Treating sleep as the first thing to sacrifice when busy.
- Using weekends to "catch up" on sleep and shifting the body clock by hours.
- Making ergonomic changes without taking movement breaks. Even a perfect chair does not replace moving.
- Giving medical, dietary or supplement advice. Coaches stay general and refer on.

## 10. Practice questions

1. Why is a consistent wake-up time often recommended before other sleep tricks? *(It helps set the body clock, which makes falling asleep and waking easier.)*
2. How often should someone stand up or move while working at a desk? *(Roughly every 30 to 60 minutes, even for a minute or two.)*
3. A person on late client calls cannot sleep until 1 am. What is one small first step? *(Build a 20 to 30 minute wind-down with no screens or work, check caffeine timing, and discuss call timing with the manager. If sleep problems persist, see a doctor.)*
